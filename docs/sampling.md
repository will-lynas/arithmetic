# Multiplication sampling

`src/scheduler.ts` derives a randomized policy from the immutable attempt ledger; it writes no state and changes no Stats metrics. It is evidence-informed, not an implementation or validation of published ARTS. [ARTS methods, pp. 2533–2534](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED549436.pdf) motivate prioritizing errors over slow correct retrievals, logarithmic latency and enforced intervening trials. Published ARTS selects maximum priority; our randomization, weights and thresholds are custom engineering choices, not measured learning benefits.

- One observation is a question presentation: `(roundId, questionIndex, left, right)`. Any wrong submission makes it failed. Repeated errors and same-screen correction cannot create additional failures or clean retrieval credit. Reversed operands retain separate evidence. Valid canceled-round attempts count; unsubmitted input does not.
- Keep the latest 256 observed presentations, at most eight per fact. A failed presentation resets error strength to `exp(-interveningTrials / 64) × exp(-ageDays / 30)`; later independent clean presentations each halve it. Clock rollback clamps age to zero. Error boost is at most 12, never a lifetime error count.
- Speed uses only first-submission active elapsed time from clean presentations. After 12 global clean observations, the latest 64 clean observations establish a median log-latency baseline. Clamp timing influence to 250–30,000 ms without changing records. Fact median log-latency above baseline contributes a boost capped at 2 (log ratio reaches its cap at 4× baseline), shrunk by `n / (n + 2)`. This is typed-submit latency, not response onset or a failure label.
- Two intervening observed presentations are required before either operand order repeats. At most two presentations of an exact pair are allowed in a ten-question round. These filters derive from full history, including across cancellation/reload/round boundaries.
- Every selection is one weighted random draw over eligible facts, proportional to `1 + coverageBoost + errorBoost + speedBoost`. There is no separate uniform branch. For completed-presentation count `c`, coverage boost is `12 × 64^(2 − c)` while `c < 3`, then zero: **49,152, 768, 12, 0** after zero, one, two, or three-plus completions. The geometric decline makes the first completion much more important than the second or third; the last step is comparable to the maximum error boost. All coefficients and the cutoff are engineering choices, not empirically optimal learning values.
- Count each independent presentation once if any submission eventually succeeds, including corrected and canceled-round questions, but not wrong-only attempts or extra retries. Correction still retains the presentation's error evidence. Derive saturated completion counts from the full validated ledger, never the recent 256-presentation difficulty window, so older completions never become unseen again. Coverage boost vanishes after the third completed presentation; ongoing error/speed adaptation and spacing still apply. The positive baseline of 1 keeps every eligible fact reachable and prevents tiny stale/recovered signals from dominating mature-fact draws. Equal eligible weights naturally produce uniform probabilities. Coverage remains probabilistic, not a guaranteed shuffled traversal or strict curriculum.

## Cold-start policy check (not learning efficacy)

Fixed-seed trajectories append real validated presentation records, use ten-question rounds and the actual difficulty calculation, enforce spacing, and correct a wrong first submission every seventh question. Comparing 24 seeds per geometric decay factor with the third-completion boost held at 12 gave:

| Decay factor | Boosts at 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+ | Median unique after 144 | Runs with ≥135 unique | Full coverage median / 95th percentile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 48 / 24 / 12 / 0 | 106 | 0/24 | 320 / 388 |
| 16 | 3,072 / 192 / 12 / 0 | 132 | 2/24 | 175 / 193 |
| 32 | 12,288 / 384 / 12 / 0 | 136 | 18/24 | 164 / 178 |
| 64 | 49,152 / 768 / 12 / 0 | 139 | 24/24 | 153 / 164 |
| 128 | 196,608 / 1,536 / 12 / 0 | 141 | 24/24 | 149 / 152 |

64 is the smallest tested factor meeting the practical first-pass targets. A separate 96-seed check of the actual sampler found **135 minimum / 139 median unique after 144**, all 96 runs at least 135; full coverage took **153 median, 161 at the 95th percentile, 167 maximum** answered presentations. These bounded synthetic results do not guarantee an individual run or establish learning benefits. Against 143 once-completed facts, the last eligible unseen fact still competes with their aggregate mass: roughly 31% next-draw probability, not certainty. Seeds and fixtures are in `src/schedulerTrajectories.test.ts`.

Within a round, question indices establish presentation order despite timestamp rollback or ties; submission elapsed-time ties retain the existing conservative wrong-first policy. Across legacy rounds there is no durable monotonic sequence: latest recorded round wall time then UUID provide a deterministic approximation, with the currently owned round last. Exact cross-round chronology after clock changes cannot be recovered from the existing ledger. Only submitted presentations are persisted, so an unsubmitted question can be resampled after reload, as before; navigation, retries, failed saves and storage refreshes never resample the displayed question.

Start and successful saves read the full persisted history; restoration uses the selected round for lifecycle identity but full history for sampling. Malformed/unsupported records are excluded by the existing validator, not repaired. Tests cover policy distributions with fixed seeds and lifecycle regressions in a real browser; they do not measure retention or prove learning efficacy.
