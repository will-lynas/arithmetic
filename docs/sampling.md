# Multiplication sampling

`src/scheduler.ts` derives a randomized policy from the immutable attempt ledger; it writes no state and changes no Stats metrics. It is evidence-informed, not an implementation or validation of published ARTS. [ARTS methods, pp. 2533–2534](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED549436.pdf) motivate prioritizing errors over slow correct retrievals, logarithmic latency and enforced intervening trials. Published ARTS selects maximum priority; our randomization, weights and thresholds are custom engineering choices, not measured learning benefits.

- One observation is a question presentation: `(roundId, questionIndex, left, right)`. Any wrong submission makes it failed. Repeated errors and same-screen correction cannot create additional failures or clean retrieval credit. Reversed operands retain separate evidence. Valid canceled-round attempts count; unsubmitted input does not.
- Keep the latest 256 observed presentations, at most eight per fact. A failed presentation resets error strength to `exp(-interveningTrials / 64) × exp(-ageDays / 30)`; later independent clean presentations each halve it. Clock rollback clamps age to zero. Error boost is at most 12, never a lifetime error count.
- Speed uses only first-submission active elapsed time from clean presentations. After 12 global clean observations, the latest 64 clean observations establish a median log-latency baseline. Clamp timing influence to 250–30,000 ms without changing records. Fact median log-latency above baseline contributes a boost capped at 2 (log ratio reaches its cap at 4× baseline), shrunk by `n / (n + 2)`. This is typed-submit latency, not response onset or a failure label.
- Two intervening observed presentations are required before either operand order repeats. At most two presentations of an exact pair are allowed in a ten-question round. These filters apply to both selection components and derive from full history, including across cancellation/reload/round boundaries.
- Half of selections are uniform random draws over eligible facts. The other half draw proportionally to `0.05 + coverageBoost + errorBoost + speedBoost`. Coverage boost is `16 × (3 − min(lifetimeCompletedPresentations, 3))`: **48, 32, 16, 0** after zero, one, two, or three-plus completions. Even one missing completion outweighs the maximum combined error/speed boost (12 + 2). This makes lifetime coverage primary, recent errors secondary and slow clean retrievals third in the weighted score, while keeping probabilistic selection rather than a strict curriculum. The coefficient, mixture and linear shape are custom engineering choices, not empirically optimal values.
- Count each independent presentation once if any submission eventually succeeds, including corrected and canceled-round questions, but not wrong-only attempts or extra retries. Correction still retains the presentation's error evidence. Derive saturated completion counts from the full validated ledger, never the recent 256-presentation difficulty window, so older completions never become unseen again. Coverage boost vanishes after the third completed presentation; ongoing error/speed adaptation and spacing still apply. The draw floor makes stale/recovered errors and timing noise lose influence rather than taking half the draws merely by being the only positive signal. With no eligible boost, use uniform coverage exclusively. No fact is permanently retired; coverage is probabilistic, not a guaranteed shuffled traversal.

Within a round, question indices establish presentation order despite timestamp rollback or ties; submission elapsed-time ties retain the existing conservative wrong-first policy. Across legacy rounds there is no durable monotonic sequence: latest recorded round wall time then UUID provide a deterministic approximation, with the currently owned round last. Exact cross-round chronology after clock changes cannot be recovered from the existing ledger. Only submitted presentations are persisted, so an unsubmitted question can be resampled after reload, as before; navigation, retries, failed saves and storage refreshes never resample the displayed question.

Start and successful saves read the full persisted history; restoration uses the selected round for lifecycle identity but full history for sampling. Malformed/unsupported records are excluded by the existing validator, not repaired. Tests cover policy distributions with fixed seeds and lifecycle regressions in a real browser; they do not measure retention or prove learning efficacy.

## Read-only Debug

`/multiplication/debug` is linked quietly below multiplication Stats. It inspects a hypothetical next draw at the displayed snapshot time, using the mounted active round's identity when one exists (otherwise fresh-round eligibility). It does not resample or predict the already chosen question. Navigation, storage events and Refresh snapshot update the view without writing ledger data or consuming randomness.

The selector and diagnostics share `questionPool`, so cooldowns and exact-pair caps have one authority. All 144 ordered pairs retain their uncapped lifetime completions and individual raw factors, including excluded pairs. Exclusions have zero chance; eligible chance is `0.5 / eligibleCount + 0.5 × totalWeight / sumEligibleWeights`. The table sorts by that combined probability, then operands. The single-random-call uniform path for equal weights remains unchanged.

Seven equal-width histograms count the whole population of 144 questions: coverage/error/speed boost, total raw weight including floor, final chance (%), first-try accuracy (%) and arithmetic-mean active solve time (seconds, including correction). Each question counts once. Zero is included; excluded questions contribute zero chance but retain their raw score factors. Unobserved accuracy and unsolved time are missing, not zero, and have explicit counts. Bins are lower-inclusive and upper-exclusive, with the final upper endpoint included. Coverage-cohort probabilities and eligible factor-score shares are separate summaries, not additional independent selection components. Synthetic acceptance fixtures exist only in tests; this page introduces no seeding, reset, migration, query-based demo mode or tuning controls.
